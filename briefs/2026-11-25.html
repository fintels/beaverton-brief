Public hearings

Hearing + vote Hotel tax would double, from 4% to 8%

Ordinance 4889 would raise Beaverton's transient lodging tax, which hotel, motel and short-term-rental guests pay, from 4% to 8% starting Jan. 1, 2027. It would also change definitions, exemptions and how the money can be used.

  • Why: the tax has raised about $22 million since 2016 and averaged $2.7 million a year over the last five years, but receipts have leveled off and are declining as business travel changes. Staff say it has only been enough for the Patricia Reser Center for the Arts (construction, debt and part of operations), so the city's arts program has needed General Fund money. The increase is part of the city's Fiscal Sustainability Plan.
  • What guests would pay: the combined rate is 14.5% today (city 4%, Washington County 9%, state 1.5%). The state share is already rising to 2.75% on Jan. 1, which brings the combined rate to 15.75% before any city change. Staff's comparison table lists Portland's combined rate at 17.25% and Hillsboro's at 14.75%.
  • Background: first reading was Nov. 17.

Agenda Bill 26188 and staff presentation (CFO Carolyn Sullivan)

Hearing + vote Civil-rights code would cover family and relationship structure

Ordinance 4890 would expand the Beaverton Civil Rights Code (Chapter 5.16) to bar discrimination in housing, employment and public accommodation based on family or relationship structure. That includes multi-partner and multi-parent families, stepfamilies, multigenerational households and consensually non-monogamous relationships. There are exceptions for employer-provided health benefits and valid conflict-of-interest policies.

  • The council asked for this on Oct. 6. Staff also updated the 2004 code to reflect current state and federal law, for example removing language saying state law "does not clearly prohibit" discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity.
  • Enforcement would not change: either a private lawsuit or the Oregon Bureau of Labor and Industries. Staff expect no cost.

Agenda Bill 26190 (City Attorney Scott Kaplan)

Hearing + vote Garbage and recycling code rewrite

Ordinance 4888 updates the city's 2002 solid waste and recycling code. It aligns definitions with the state's Recycling Modernization Act, revises how hauler franchises are granted, renewed and reviewed, and changes the rule on how high trees must be trimmed above roadways. Two resolutions would set a fee for franchise transfer applications and adopt a franchise service-area map. The council held a work session on Aug. 18.

Agenda Bill 26189

Action item

Vote Should Beaverton bid to host the LA28 Olympic torch relay?

The Olympic flame will pass through all 50 states before the Los Angeles opening ceremony on July 14, 2028, and Beaverton has been invited to be considered for a route. The council will decide whether the city manager can sign an agreement committing the city's willingness to participate. That's not a guarantee Beaverton is selected.

  • Estimated cost: $75,000 to $150,000 for day-of support on a 1- to 3-mile route: police escort and crowd control, street closures, emergency medical service through TVF&R, sanitation, accessibility and communications. That excludes staff planning time.
  • The city would have to provide this support at no charge to LA28 and waive its own permit fees. Staff say costs could be offset by mutual aid, sponsors or one-time hotel tax money. No date has been set.

Appeal hearing

Hearing + decision Vehicle storage yard at 3640 SW 110th Ave.

The city's Community Development Director approved a vehicle storage yard next to the existing Damerow Ford dealership (applicant KIPB, LLC) on Oct. 15. It includes five 30-foot pole lights, a six-foot solid wood fence and landscaping on the north side, and a new driveway connecting the yard to the dealership to the south. The next-door neighbor to the north filed the only appeal on Oct. 26. The appeal does not seek to stop the storage use.

  • What the commission can do: the hearing is "de novo," so the commission may reconsider any part of the design review, not only the points raised. A separate tree-pruning approval was not appealed and is off the table.
  • Lighting and glare: the appellant says the decision doesn't show the lights are shielded from second-story windows. Staff say the plans show no observable shielding and recommend a new condition: before the site development permit, the applicant must provide details showing light shields that minimize glare to neighboring homes. Staff say the code has no basis to require dimming lights after hours, as the appellant asked.
  • Headlights on the south driveway: the applicant has reduced the driveway grade from 10.1% to 9.5% and submitted a drawing showing headlights aimed at the fence and landscaping. Staff agree and recommend a condition locking in a grade of no more than 9.5%.
  • Not recommended: conditions on gate use at SW 110th, car-carrier deliveries and the scope of activity in the yard. Staff found no code deficiency. On noise, staff say the city noise code applies separately and is not an approval criterion. Staff confirmed the north landscape buffer is 12.8 feet, as on the approved plans.
  • Staff recommendation: add the two conditions, otherwise deny the appeal. No public comment from anyone other than the appellant, and no agency comment, had been received when the memo was published Nov. 25.
  • Next: adoption of the final order is on the commission's Dec. 16 look-ahead.
